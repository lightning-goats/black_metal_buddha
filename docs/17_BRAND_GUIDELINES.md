# Black Metal Buddha — Brand Identity Guidelines

**Status:** Approved baseline  
**Approved logo asset:** `app/static/brand/black-metal-buddha-logo.webp`

These rules apply to the website, packaging, marketplace listings, social graphics, future apparel, and generated design work.

## Brand idea

Black Metal Buddha combines the stark visual language of black metal with contemplative Buddhist themes: impermanence, mortality, non-self, awareness, and release.

The tone should feel:

- austere
- dark
- contemplative
- severe
- handcrafted / distressed
- intelligent rather than campy
- reverent toward Buddhist source material
- visually aggressive without becoming visually incoherent

The brand is **not** generic occult merchandise and should not become a collage of unrelated religious symbols.

---

## Approved primary mark

The approved primary mark consists of:

- a recognizable **Dzogchen A** interpreted in a black-metal visual language
- a sparse spiked circular/halo form
- the stylized `Black Metal Buddha` wordmark
- bone-white distressed ink on black
- very restrained muted-red accents

The current approved website asset is:

```
app/static/brand/black-metal-buddha-logo.webp
```

The master image should remain archived separately at the highest available resolution when production/vector cleanup is performed later.

### What made the approved direction work

- clear central Dzogchen A
- strong negative space inside the circle
- few interior decorative elements
- sharp silhouette
- limited palette
- enough distress to connect with the shirt artwork
- wordmark can be highly stylized because the site presents readable brand text beside it

# Religious-symbol guardrail

## No Christian iconography

**Do not introduce Christian crosses or cross-like ornaments into Black Metal Buddha branding or artwork.**

This includes accidental forms created by:

- a long vertical spike extending below a horizontal bar
- centered ornamental dagger/cross constructions
- cruciform separators
- inverted crosses
- Celtic/Gothic crosses
- cross finials borrowed from church architecture
- decorative symbols that read primarily as a Christian cross at normal viewing size

The approved logo was specifically revised to remove a lower central point that caused a cross-like reading.

When reviewing future artwork, judge the **silhouette at normal size**, not merely the designer's intent.

If an element can reasonably read as Christian iconography, redesign it.

## Preferred symbolic vocabulary

Use selectively and accurately:

- Dzogchen A
- lotus
- dharma wheel where contextually appropriate
- moon / sun / eclipse
- emptiness / void imagery
- mala / prayer beads
- Buddhist stupas and temple architecture
- ravens/crows as atmospheric imagery
- skeletal / charnel-ground imagery where thematically appropriate
- flame / smoke
- geometric radial forms that do not become cross-shaped

Do not indiscriminately mix symbols from unrelated traditions merely because they look “occult.”

- black should dominate
- bone-white should carry most information
- red is an accent, not a background theme

- avoid metallic gold unless explicitly introduced by a later approved identity revision
- product artwork may contain richer tonal variation, but interface chrome should remain restrained

# Typography

## Functional text must be human-readable

All interface and content text must prioritize comprehension.

This includes:

- navigation
- headings
- product names
- descriptions
- pricing
- size selection
- checkout
- policies
- FAQs
- buttons
- errors
- transactional email
- SEO-visible copy

Current website direction:

- readable serif display type for major headings
- readable system/sans-serif text for body/UI
- generous letter spacing used sparingly for labels and eyebrows

## Black-metal typography is reserved for

- the approved logo/wordmark
- artwork printed on merchandise
- rare decorative graphic moments that are repeated immediately in readable text if they carry meaning

Never use an illegible black-metal font for essential navigation, product information, or checkout.

---

# Distress and texture

Distress should make an element feel printed, worn, or physical—not merely noisy.

Prefer:

- subtle ink loss
- sparse speckles
- rough printed edges
- restrained scratches
- screen-print / photocopy character

Avoid:

- texture that destroys small text
- random visual noise over every surface
- excessive dripping UI decoration
- effects that compromise accessibility
- tiny ornamental clutter added only to make something “more metal”

The approved logo is intentionally **starker** than the first concepts.

---

# Layout language

Prefer:

- large negative space
- strong central compositions
- symmetry where appropriate
- thin restrained borders
- deliberate red accents
- large readable editorial headings
- product art as the visual focus

Avoid:

- busy occult collage layouts
- dense filigree behind functional UI
- gratuitous spikes on buttons/inputs
- ornamental separators that resemble religious crosses
- visual treatments that compete with the shirts

The merchandise should remain more elaborate than the ecommerce interface.

---

# Logo usage

## Header

Use the approved mark at compact size with readable `BLACK METAL BUDDHA` text adjacent to it.

The image may have empty alt text in this case because the adjacent readable name supplies the accessible label and the link itself has an accessible name.

## Hero / standalone use

When the logo is presented without adjacent readable text, provide an accessible text equivalent.

## Clear space

Maintain breathing room around the logo. Do not let navigation, borders, or artwork touch its outer spikes.

As a working rule, preserve clear space of at least roughly **10% of the logo's rendered width** around the full mark.

## Minimum size

The full wordmark lockup loses useful detail when made very small.

For tiny uses (favicon/social avatar), a later simplified icon-only Dzogchen-A mark should be produced from the same approved identity rather than continually shrinking the full lockup.

Until that icon is approved, the current asset may be used as a temporary favicon.

## Do not

- recolor into rainbow palettes
- add glow/neon effects
- stretch or skew
- add bevel/chrome effects
- add extra rays inside the circle
- reintroduce the removed lower cross-like point
- surround with extra pseudo-occult sigils
- place over a visually busy image without sufficient contrast

# Product-art relationship

The website identity and shirt artwork should feel related but not identical.

The shirt art can be:

- denser
- more illustrative
- more distressed
- more narrative
- more typographically extreme

The site should act as a dark gallery around that artwork.

A good test:

> If the interface itself looks like another T-shirt graphic, it is probably too ornate.

---

# Buddhist-source integrity

Black Metal Buddha uses Buddhist philosophical themes as more than surface decoration.

When product copy discusses Buddhist concepts:

- use ordinary accepted meanings
- distinguish a specific teaching/tradition when relevant
- avoid claiming lineage or authority the brand does not have
- do not fabricate quotes from teachers or scriptures
- avoid mixing Buddhist symbols with unrelated religious symbolism for visual convenience
- prefer explanatory language over mystical marketing claims

The site may be irreverent in black-metal tone without misrepresenting Buddhist teachings.

---

# Review checklist for new visual work

Before approving a new asset, ask:

- [ ] Does it still read as Black Metal Buddha without relying on the name?
- [ ] Is the Buddhist symbol being used intentionally?
- [ ] Are there any accidental Christian/cross forms?
- [ ] Is the silhouette strong at thumbnail size?
- [ ] Is there enough negative space?
- [ ] Has unnecessary ornament been removed?
- [ ] Are red accents restrained?
- [ ] Is all functional text easy to read?
- [ ] Does the design complement rather than compete with the apparel art?
- [ ] Would the element still work as a one- or two-color print?
- [ ] Does it avoid generic occult-symbol collage?

If in doubt, simplify.
